      The philosophy

      The art of wabi-sabi

      Wabi-sabi finds beauty in what is plain, worn, uneven and unfinished. In this studio it is a working rule. Nothing gets sanded back to match its neighbour.

      Where the glaze ran, it stays.

      See how a piece is made
      Uneven is honest
      A rim that dips on one side shows the hand that made it. Symmetry is not the goal, so it is not forced.
      The kiln gets a vote
      Glaze pools, breaks over edges and drips. Iron speckles burn through cream and blue. Each firing writes its own marks.
      Slow on purpose
      Weeks pass between a lump of clay and a finished piece. Drying cannot be hurried, and some pieces warp or crack. The ones that make it are the ones you see here.
      Made to be used
      Olives in a dip bowl, a ring in a trinket dish, one stem in a bud vase. Wear is part of the plan.

        Looking after it

        Washing

        Wash by hand in warm water with a soft sponge. Handmade glazes and impressed patterns last longer away from the dishwasher.

        Heat and cold

        Bring a piece to room temperature before hot food or drink. Avoid sudden swings, such as fridge to hot oven.

        Food and microwave

        Check the notes on each Etsy listing before using a piece for food or in a microwave.

        Bud vases

        Fill with fresh water for a day or two, then empty and dry the vase. The bare clay foot can hold moisture.

        What is normal

        Glaze drips, speckles, small colour shifts and a slight wobble are part of the piece. A chip or crack on arrival is not, so write to the studio.
